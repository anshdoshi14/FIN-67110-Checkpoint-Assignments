## Overview

Costco Wholesale Corporation runs membership-based warehouses and e-commerce platforms worldwide, focusing on offering a limited assortment of nationally-branded and private-label products at low prices to drive high sales volume and fast inventory turnover. Its warehouses average about 148,000 square feet, optimized for efficient merchandise handling and inventory control in a no-frills, self-service format that reduces labor and shrinkage costs. The company emphasizes volume purchasing, cross-docked distribution from depots to warehouses, and a focused SKU count of under 4,000 per warehouse and 9,000 to 11,000 online to maintain low prices. In addition to core warehouse sales, Costco operates ancillary services including gas stations, pharmacies, and travel offerings. At fiscal year-end 2026, Costco reported 84.1 million paid members globally with a US and Canada member renewal rate of 92.3%. Executive members, who pay additional fees and receive rewards, totaled 42.3 million in 2026 and accounted for about 75.2% of worldwide net sales. The company's workforce numbered 355,000 employees worldwide, predominantly in warehouses and distribution operations. ## Market Positioning and Growth Outlook

Costco’s competitive strength lies in its membership warehouse model, leveraging purchasing scale and operational efficiency to deliver consistently low prices. Its streamlined SKU selection reduces complexity and operating costs. As of fiscal 2026, Costco operated 939 warehouses worldwide, including 647 in the U.S. and Puerto Rico and 115 in Canada; combined U.S. and Canadian operations represented 86% of net sales and 83% of operating income. California alone accounted for 26% of U.S. net sales, underscoring regional concentration risk. Ancillary businesses, notably 774 gas stations, contributed 11% of total net sales, enhancing member frequency and convenience. Digital sales showed robust growth at 20.9% in fiscal 2026, signaling notable e-commerce expansion. Key growth drivers include new warehouse openings in existing and new markets, e-commerce platform broadening, and private-label portfolio growth. Challenges encompass difficulty securing suitable sites, regulatory hurdles, incremental member overlap at new locations, and competitive dynamics in international markets. Supply chain diversification and increased in-country sourcing are strategic priorities. ## Future Developments and Strategic Recommendations

Costco should prioritize expanding its warehouse network, particularly in high-potential existing markets, while vigilantly managing site acquisition risks and navigating regulatory environments. Accelerating e-commerce capabilities and fulfillment investments will be critical to capture shifting shopping preferences. Expanding private-label merchandise could boost margins and deepen member loyalty. The company must monitor geographic and product concentration risks, especially its heavy reliance on U.S. and Canadian markets and California-specific exposure. Sustaining strong membership renewal rates through competitive value propositions, including Executive rewards, will protect the stable revenue base. Operational cost control through supply chain diversification and labor management is essential. Additionally, assessing the performance and adapting strategies in new international markets will guide successful global growth.
